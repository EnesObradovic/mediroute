How to Verify Turkish Plastic Surgeon Credentials: Step-by-Step Guide

Emma Clarke 5 Haziran 2026 8 dk okuma
İçindekiler

    For UK patients considering plastic surgery, Turkey represents a highly attractive destination, offering procedures like rhinoplasty, facelifts, and high-volume liposuction at a fraction of the cost of UK private care. However, the sheer volume of marketing messages from Turkish clinics can make it difficult to identify genuinely qualified surgeons. In the UK, checking a surgeon's registration is straightforward via the General Medical Council (GMC) register. Navigating the Turkish medical system requires understanding a different set of regulatory registries and professional medical associations.

    A common mistake is selecting a clinic based on social media reviews or package prices, without verifying the specific surgeon who will perform the procedure. In Turkey, a medical doctor is licensed to practice medicine upon graduation, but they must undergo extensive specialized residency training to legally perform plastic and reconstructive surgery. Failing to verify these credentials exposes patients to unqualified general practitioners or "cosmetic doctors" operating outside their scope. This guide provides a step-by-step verification protocol to verify the training, licensing, and professional standings of any Turkish surgeon using TTB, EBOPRAS, and ISAPS registries.

    Step 1: Navigating the Turkish Medical Association (TTB) Registry

    The first step in verifying a surgeon is confirming they are licensed to practice medicine in Turkey and have completed specialized residency training. The official governing medical body in Turkey is the Turkish Medical Association (Türk Tabipleri Birliği or TTB). All practicing physicians in Turkey must be registered with the TTB and their local medical chamber (e.g., İstanbul Tabip Odası for Istanbul or İzmir Tabip Odası for Izmir).

    To verify a physician's active registration, you can use the official portals or request direct verification:

    • Official Portal Access: The Turkish Ministry of Health provides a doctor database search tool through the government portal e-Devlet (T.C. Sağlık Bakanlığı Doktor Sorgulama). While this portal requires Turkish national credentials to access directly, you can ask your clinic coordinator to provide a live screen share or an official PDF export of the surgeon's Ministry of Health registry profile.
    • Locating the License Number: Every licensed doctor in Turkey has a unique 6-digit diploma registry number (Diploma Tescil Numarası) issued by the Ministry of Health. Your clinic must provide this number upon request. If they hesitate or refuse, this is a major warning sign.
    • Verification via Local Chambers: You can contact the Istanbul Medical Chamber (İstanbul Tabip Odası) via email at their international patient inquiry address to verify a doctor's registration. They can confirm if the doctor is currently licensed and if they have any active malpractice or disciplinary investigations against them.
    "In my years auditing medical travel clinics, the single biggest risk factor for UK patients is the 'ghost surgeon' phenomenon. This is where a well-known surgeon's name is on the clinic door, but the actual operation is performed by a junior resident or a general practitioner. By demanding the surgeon's TTB registration and diploma registry number before you fly, and verifying that the same individual signs your pre-op consent forms, you protect yourself from this risk."

    Step 2: Understanding Specialization Titles in Turkey

    It is crucial to distinguish between a general practitioner and a board-certified plastic surgeon. In Turkey, medical education consists of 6 years of basic medical school, after which graduates receive the title of "Tıp Doktoru" (Medical Doctor). However, they are not yet plastic surgeons.

    To legally perform plastic, reconstructive, and cosmetic surgeries, a doctor must pass the highly competitive national medical specialty exam (TUS) and complete a rigorous 6-year residency training program specifically in plastic surgery. Upon completion, the Ministry of Health issues a specialist diploma with the exact title: "Plastik, Rekonstrüktif ve Estetik Cerrahi Uzmanı" (Plastic, Reconstructive, and Aesthetic Surgery Specialist).

    ⚠️ Warning: Beware of misleading titles: Watch out for titles like "Medikal Estetik Hekimi" (Medical Aesthetic Physician) or "Estetik Uzmanı" (Aesthetic Specialist). These doctors are often general practitioners who have taken short weekend courses in Botox or dermal fillers. They are not plastic surgeons and are not legally permitted to perform invasive surgical procedures like rhinoplasty, liposuction, or breast augmentation.

    Additionally, you should verify if the surgeon is a member of the national professional society: TPRECD (Türk Plastik, Rekonstrüktif ve Estetik Cerrahi Derneği). Membership in TPRECD requires active board certification and adherence to the national code of medical ethics. You can verify membership by searching the directory on the official TPRECD website (www.tpcd.org.tr).

    Step 3: Verifying European Board (EBOPRAS) Certification

    For UK patients seeking an extra layer of quality assurance, looking for a surgeon with European board certification is highly recommended. The leading European body is EBOPRAS (European Board of Plastic, Reconstructive and Aesthetic Surgery).

    EBOPRAS certification indicates that a plastic surgeon has met rigorous European standards, passing a comprehensive two-part examination (written and oral) covering the entire spectrum of reconstructive and aesthetic surgery. To verify a surgeon's EBOPRAS status:

    1. Visit the official EBOPRAS website (www.ebopras.org).
    2. Navigate to the "Members" or "Board Certified Surgeons" directory.
    3. Search by country ("Turkey") and filter by the surgeon's last name.
    4. Surgeons who have successfully passed the exam are officially registered as "EBOPRAS Fellows" and are permitted to use the post-nominal letters FEBOPRAS.

    Step 4: Verifying International Society (ISAPS) Membership

    Another key credential is membership in ISAPS (International Society of Aesthetic Plastic Surgery). ISAPS is the world's leading professional body for board-certified plastic surgeons, dedicated to safety and education in aesthetic surgery.

    ISAPS membership is not open to all doctors. To join, a surgeon must:

    • Hold active board certification as a plastic surgeon in their home country.
    • Be a member in good standing of their national plastic surgery society (TPRECD in Turkey).
    • Be sponsored by two active ISAPS members who vouch for their ethical standing and clinical competence.
    • Maintain continuous medical education (CME) credits in aesthetic surgery.

    You can verify a surgeon's ISAPS membership by using the "Find a Surgeon" search tool on the official ISAPS website (www.isaps.org).

    Warning Signs: Red Flags to Watch For

    When evaluating a clinic or surgeon, keep an eye out for these potential red flags:

    • The "Clinic-First" Approach: If a clinic's website, brochures, and sales agents focus heavily on the luxury hotel, VIP transfers, and clinic branding, but refuse to name the specific surgeon who will perform your procedure until you arrive in Turkey, do not book. You have a legal right to know your surgeon's identity and verify their credentials before paying a deposit.
    • Missing or Fake Badges: Some clinics place logos of EBOPRAS, ISAPS, or the TTB on their websites without actual membership. Always cross-reference the surgeon's name directly in the registries mentioned above, rather than relying on logos displayed on the clinic's homepage.
    • "Cosmetic Surgeon" vs "Plastic Surgeon": In Turkey, as in the UK, anyone with a basic medical degree can call themselves a "cosmetic surgeon." Only those who have completed the official residency are entitled to call themselves a "Plastic, Reconstructive, and Aesthetic Surgery Specialist" (Plastik, Rekonstrüktif ve Estetik Cerrahi Uzmanı).
