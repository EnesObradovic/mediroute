Technology Check: Verify the Laser Equipment

The success of refractive surgery depends heavily on the technology platform used. The best specialized eye centers in Istanbul use the same hardware found in London Harley Street clinics. Before selecting a clinic, confirm the laser systems they use:

1. Excimer Laser Platforms (For reshaping the corneal bed)

  • Alcon WaveLight EX500: The fastest excimer laser available, operating at 500 Hz. It features multi-dimensional eye-tracking and wavefront-optimized treatment profiles to minimize the risk of night glares.
  • Johnson & Johnson VISX Star S4 IR: Features Iris Registration technology to adjust the laser pattern for subtle eye movements during treatment.

2. Femtosecond Laser Platforms (For creating the corneal flap in LASIK)

  • Carl Zeiss VisuMax: Known for its precision, it is the only laser system capable of performing the ReLEx SMILE procedure, which uses a keyhole extraction method.
  • Alcon WaveLight FS200: Creates precise corneal flaps in approximately 6 seconds, reducing patient discomfort.

A leading surgeon in Istanbul may perform over 1,500 procedures annually, compared to 200 to 400 for a typical UK private surgeon. This high volume supports consistent, precise results when utilizing advanced laser systems."

— Dr. Burcu Arslan, Refractive & Corneal Surgery Specialist

Post-Operative Care and Flying Home Safely

While laser eye surgery is a quick outpatient procedure, the return flight requires preparation to protect your healing corneas:

⚠️ Wait Before Flying: Do not fly for at least 48 hours (for LASIK/SMILE) or 5 days (for LASEK) after surgery. The dry air and pressure changes in the aircraft cabin can cause severe dry eyes, which can delay healing or cause flap issues if the eyes are rubbed.

Travel Protocols for UK Patients:

  • Lubrication: Use preservative-free artificial tears every 15 to 30 minutes during the flight. Cabin humidity is low, which accelerates tear evaporation.
  • Eye Protection: Wear the provided protective sunglasses inside the terminal and aircraft. This shields your eyes from dust, dry air vents, and bright lights.
  • No Rubbing: The corneal flap takes several weeks to fully bond. Avoid rubbing your eyes, and use the protective eye shields during sleep for the first week.
  • Avoid Screens: Rest your eyes during the flight and limit screen time on tablets or phones to reduce dry eye symptoms.
